# Week 6 - PREP Under Pressure
*Phase 2: Spontaneous Speaking | 2026-06-15 to 2026-06-19*

## Key Topic
Using PREP when genuinely unprepared - responding to unexpected questions from senior leaders without freezing or over-explaining.

## Key Concepts Covered
- The 3-second pause rule: always pause before responding, especially under pressure
- PREP mock sessions with Claude: simulating real senior leader questioning
- Resisting the urge to fill silence with filler words or over-context

## PREP Practice Session - 2026-06-15 (Mon, Day 26)

### Q4: What's the one decision you've made this quarter that you'd defend hardest?

**Your answer:**
> I decide to, uh, terminate my offer for two properties that recommended by buyer's agent. It's because they both have a significant risk of structure engineering defects. In addition, we were not able to assess the survey to identify the potential signs of risk and the cost of repairs - without understanding the cost of repairs could go from couple of thousands to close to one hundred thousand dollars. For the first investment property, I'm not able to take that risk to get up even a bargain there. Therefore, I reject it.

**Evaluation:**
| Element | Result | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Point | Present, slow | "I decide to, uh" - filler kills the opening. Try: *"I walked away from two properties my buyer's agent recommended."* |
| Reason | Solid | Structural risk + inability to assess cost is clear and defensible |
| Example | Weak | Cost range ($2K-$100K) is good, but no specific moment or timeline |
| Closing Point | Weak | "Therefore, I reject it" is abrupt. Try: *"Without that number, I couldn't justify the risk - so I walked away."* |

**One improvement:** Lead with a declarative statement, not a process. *"I walked away - and I'd do it again."* Then explain why.

### Q15: What would you do differently if you started this year over?

**Your answer:**
> I would have started learning new leadership communication skills earlier and invested more time on it. Even though I've started this year, I have been slow taking courses, looking for the ways to learn rather than starting to learn. The best way to learn is to start with anything that you can so you can take a step by step. My key takeaway from this experience is that if you want to do something, start with something rather than looking for the ways to start.

**Evaluation:**
| Element | Result | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Point | Strong | Clear and immediate - came first with no hesitation |
| Reason | Present, slightly buried | "I spent too long planning how to learn instead of learning" - tighten this line |
| Example | Missing | No specific moment. Try: *"I had Think Faster, Talk Smarter on my list for three months before I opened it."* |
| Closing Point | Strong | *"Start with something rather than looking for ways to start"* - memorable, quotable, executive-level. Keep this exactly. |

**One improvement:** Add one concrete moment in the Example slot - a specific book, month, or delayed conversation. The closing line is excellent; it just needs a scene before it.

### Q11: If I gave you one extra senior engineer for 3 months, where would you put them?

**Your answer:**
> I would put them on the data ingestion and data modeling for Marketo on the Cultural New Platform applications, because these are new applications. The platform integration must be thoroughly considered with a CRM, and we don't have enough subject matter exposed to otherwise. Without them, it will take significant effort and time even though they are highly skilled engineers. Therefore, dedicated senior engineer is highly required for this project.

**Evaluation:**
| Element | Result | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Point | Strong | Specific, immediate, no filler - best opening of the session |
| Reason | Solid | CRM integration complexity + expertise gap is clear. Fix: "subject matter **exposed**" should be "subject matter **expertise**" |
| Example | Missing | Try: *"Right now we're estimating 6 months - a senior engineer with CRM experience could cut that in half."* |
| Closing Point | Weak | "Highly required" is unusual phrasing. Try: *"Without that person, this becomes our biggest delivery risk."* |

**One improvement:** Lock in fixed phrases under pressure - "subject matter **expertise**", "business impact", "key dependency." These get dropped or swapped when you're rushed. Drill them as fixed units.

### Session Summary

| | Q4 (Property decision) | Q15 (Start year over) | Q11 (Senior engineer) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Point first | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Reason clear | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Example | Weak | Missing | Missing |
| Closing Point | Weak | Strong | Weak |

**Pattern to work on:** Point and Reason are becoming reliable. The **Example slot is consistently thin** - you're giving context instead of a scene. One number, one moment, one name is all it takes.
