# Week 7 — PREP Under Pressure
*Phase 2: Spontaneous Speaking | 2026-06-01 to 2026-06-05*

## Key Topic
Using PREP when genuinely unprepared — responding to unexpected questions from senior leaders without freezing or over-explaining.

## Key Concepts Covered
- The 3-second pause rule: always pause before responding, especially under pressure
- PREP mock sessions with Claude: simulating real senior leader questioning
- Resisting the urge to fill silence with filler words or over-context

## Examples from Practice

| # | Your original | Improved version | What changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "We won't know whether this is blocking our AI — it's technically something we as a team need to address the risk of and work with other teams to investigate." | "My view is that we're not blocked. I'll speak to those two teams to find out what data quality issues they're concerned about and get back to you by end of day." | Led with a clear Point instead of explaining uncertainty. Added a specific commitment deadline. Removed filler words (um, uh). |

**Pressure simulation context (2026-06-14):**
Scenario: COO in AI Steering Committee asks mid-meeting — "I've been hearing from two different teams that our data quality is blocking the AI rollout. But I've also heard from your team that we're on track. Someone's wrong. Which is it — and what are you going to do about it?"

- **Response 1 (weak):** Started with process steps and investigation — no position stated, heavy filler words, evasive
- **Response 2 (pushback round):** COO pressed for one sentence. Still listed actions rather than leading with a stance
- **Response 3 (breakthrough):** Used "My view is..." anchor → immediately produced a clear Point + action + deadline in one sentence

**Key insight:** The instinct under pressure is to explain before concluding. Forcing "My view is..." at the start short-circuits that default and commits to a position before hedging can begin.
