**Day 2 (2026-04-28) — *Speak Up* podcast takeaways**

Three principles for any spoken contribution:
1. **Audience first** — what do they need from this, not what do I want to say
2. **Clarity and interest** — strip out the noise, find the compelling angle
3. **Add a recommendation** — move from informing to leading

Default speaking order: **goal → problem → solution**

"So I'm working on the engagement signals project." — "So" + filler opening
"Our goal for this year is to deliver…" — soft, descriptive rather than directive

**Day 5 (2026-05-01) — Reflection on hedging with close colleagues**

Reviewed vocab bank and noticed I use "I think", "sorry but", "maybe", "we should do X", "I might be wrong but" constantly — including with my team and close stakeholders. Question I sat with: is it okay to hedge with people I'm close to, or should I always speak the leader way to keep the habit?

Decision: drop the hedges in *all* work settings, close colleagues included.

Reasoning:
- These phrases aren't "casual" — they're self-undermining. They lower the perceived confidence of my view regardless of audience.
- Reflexes don't switch on by audience. If I hedge 8 hours a day with my team, the habit will leak through under pressure in the AI Steering Committee.
- Warmth ≠ hedging. I can stay collaborative and human through tone, asking for input, and acknowledging others — not by burying my point.

Replacements I'm adopting:
- "what's your take?"
- "happy to be challenged on this"
- "let's figure this out together"
- "my view is X — does that line up with yours?"

Practical rule: treat "sorry but / I might be wrong but / maybe / I think maybe" as banned in any work setting. Save real "sorry" for when I've actually done something wrong.
